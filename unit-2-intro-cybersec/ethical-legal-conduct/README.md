### Scenario 1: The colleague's password

**What is happening:**
On the one side, a colleague asks me to log into his account, and I can do so based on acceptable use policy 
"Personal use of work IT is generally tolerated but monitored", but on the other side, my colleague is violating 
a rule from that same policy "Don't share credentials with anyone, even colleagues, even temporarily".

**What's legally at stake:**
If I log in as her and perform an action, the system legally records her as the actor. This could result in a 
mandatory data breach notification and significant fines being imposed on the organisation by the regulatory authorities.


**What's ethically at stake:**
It turns out that we’re both breaking the law, even though the client thinks everything’s being done properly on the other 
side. If I refuse to log into the account, I’ll probably undermine my credibility in her eyes, but on the other hand, we’re 
secretly breaking the rules.

**What I would do:**
I would probably ask my colleague to put the call on hold for a moment and ask her to log into the account herself. I 
would explain to her that we’d be breaking the law if we did what she wants.

**What I would NOT do, and why:**
I wouldn’t log into her account or look at the ticket myself. If I do that, I will probably breach the confidentiality of 
both my colleague’s data and the client’s data.

**Who I would consult:**
I would consult with team lead or supervisor. I would describe the situation hypothetically, without mentioning any real people, 
and ask what to do in such a situation.

### Scenario 2: The found credentials

**What is happening:**
When I’m about to change keyboards, I find a colleague’s account password on the old one. My colleague is not far from me, but he can’t 
see what’s going on. 

**What's legally at stake:**
Essentially, I'm not breaking any laws by not using the password I received for my own purposes. 
I just found the sticker and that's all. But if I use his password for my own purposes, I violate acceptable use policy
"Don't access systems you don't need access to, even if you technically can".

**What's ethically at stake:**
I can tell my coworker that I found a sticker with his password so he knows about my discovery, and explain that I don't plan to use it.
Then he might realize that I'm not violating his privacy and that I'm someone he can trust.

**What I would do:**
I'll go over to my coworker, tell him about my discovery, and explain how I found it. I'll explain that I want to replace the keyboard 
and suggest that he write his password on the new sticker.

**What I would NOT do, and why:**
I won't use his password to gain access to the admin account. I won't keep my discovery a secret.

**Who I would consult:**
I would consult with my colleague. We would discuss this issue together and come to a decision.

### Scenario 3: The personal data peek

**What is happening:**
A colleague of mine from another department has asked me to review her HR files—these are files that contain employees' personal information, 
reports, schedules, and so on. And I have access to those files.

**What's legally at stake:**
Essentially, if I were to look through her files, I would be breaching the GDPR, because the data my friend has asked to see is classified as 
personal data. So it turns out that even though I have access to her data, I still can't view it, and doing so is considered as snooping.

**What's ethically at stake:**
If I don't grant her request, she'll probably be upset because it's important to her, but in that case, I won't be breaking the law. She probably 
doesn't understand the seriousness of the situation, and I'm putting both her company's and my own reputation at risk.

**What I would do:**
I can refuse her request on the grounds of the GDPR. I would suggest that she contact the HR department so that it's done officially and doesn't violate the law.

**What I would NOT do, and why:**
I wouldn't look through her files because I'm basically looking at her personal files without her permission, and that's a crime.

**Who I would consult:**
I would talk to the HR director and ask what my friend can do in this situation.

### Scenario 4 - The vulnerability you accidentally noticed

**What is happening:**
I’ve found a vulnerability on my work website where, by changing just one digit, I can access another person’s personal data.
I close the tab and nobody noticed.

**What's legally at stake:**
I breached the GDPR by disclosing another user’s confidentiality. Although nobody noticed.

**What's ethically at stake:**
I have an ethical duty to take steps to prevent similar situations from arising in future. From now on, I am responsible for my 
company’s data, and if I ignore this situation, it could present a golden opportunity for hackers.

**What I would do:**
I have to report the vulnerability responsibly to company’s IT, security department, or management. I have to say something like, 
"While testing the website, I discovered such-and-such a vulnerability...".

**What I would NOT do, and why:**
I am not ignoring the situation, and under no circumstances do I use the data I have received for my own purposes.

**Who I would consult:**
I consulting with company’s IT, security department, or management.

### Scenario 5 - The off-hours request

**What is happening:**
A friend, knowing that I’m interested in cybersecurity in my spare time, has asked me to take a look at a vulnerability on his website. 
Although I’m probably not qualified in the field of cybersecurity, he’s still asking for my help.

**What's legally at stake:**
I could violate Finnish criminal law (Chapter 38, Section 8 of the Criminal Code) on unauthorized access doing whatever my friend wants. 

**What's ethically at stake:**
I’ll be putting my reputation and our friendship at risk if I agree to his request and end up causing any trouble.

**What I would do:**
I would politely decline and suggest they contact IT support for help. 

**What I would NOT do, and why:**
I wouldn’t have agreed to my friend’s request and wouldn’t have helped him.

**Who I would consult:**
I would ask the IT support team for help on behalf of my friend. Or I would have asked a friend to contact IT support
