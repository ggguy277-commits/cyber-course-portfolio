# Attacker vs Defender — [mobile phone]

## The system
[1–2 sentences: what it is and why it matters to you]
- A modern smartphone is basically the central hub for whole digital life, carrying bank accounts, socials, private photos etc.
If someone has stoled my phone and knows the lockscreen code, they essentially hold my entire digital identity.

## Attacker view
[3–5 sentences: what, how, what they gain]
- If hacker has physical access and lockscreen passcode, he will change account passwords via email or SMS.
Or basically he can enter any messenger on my phone and send fishing sites to friends and parents and
he might be able to find out more about me just by looking through my messages. His motivation may be
that there is bank's app and the Suomi.fi app downloaded on my phone.

## Defender view
[3–5 sentences: what defense, how it works, what attacker must change, what it doesn't cover]
- So to defend I may use fingerprints on a regular basis, so no one will see how I type my passcode,
Face ID, or extra PIN codes inside important apps. This means knowing the passcode is not enough to get into sensitive apps.
But if attacker, for example my girlfriend, she can unlock my phone by placing my finger on it while I'm sleeping,
and same with Face ID.


## CIA angle
[1–2 sentences: which leg is primarily threatened]
- Confidentiality is the main problem because an attacker can see all your private photos, texts, and bank info.
Integrity is also at risk because they can change your settings and passwords.

## What I'm changing this week
[2–3 sentences]
- I will set different passwords on different accounts. I will set up a PIN lock on my SIM card so no one can use it in another phone.
I will also turn on extra protection features like Face ID or Fingerprint lock for all my banking and password apps.
